## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Chapter 7: Compliance Carbon Markets.** Full draft, ~7,300 words. Seven parts covering cap-and-trade fundamentals; the EU ETS in depth (Phases 1-4, ETS2, 2026 reform); the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM, full enforcement Q1 2026 at €75.36/tCO₂e); North American systems (California Cap-and-Invest extended to 2045 in Sept 2025, RGGI, Washington); other major systems (China national ETS, UK ETS with pending EU linkage, Korea, others); the case for and against cap-and-trade; and cap-and-trade vs. carbon taxes.
- **Chapter 8: Voluntary Carbon Markets.** Full draft, ~7,000 words. Seven parts covering VCM fundamentals and how it differs from compliance; the project lifecycle and four integrity concerns (additionality, baseline, permanence, leakage); the major standards (Verra, Gold Standard, ART, ACR, CAR, Isometric, Puro.earth, Plan Vivo); the 2022–2024 crisis (Guardian/Die Zeit/SourceMaterial investigation, cookstove crisis, wildfire reversals); supply-side reform via ICVCM and Core Carbon Principles; demand-side reform via VCMI Claims Code and SBTi V2; current state of prices, demand, and unresolved questions about scale-vs-quality and Article 6.
- **Chapter 5: Emissions Accounting — The Rosetta Stone.** Full draft, ~8,600 words. Seven parts covering Scope 1/2/3 architecture, organizational boundaries, inventories vs. targets vs. claims, financed and facilitated emissions (PCAF), the avoided/reduced/removed distinction, current status of all major standards (GHG Protocol revisions, ISSB IFRS S1/S2 adoption, PCAF 3rd edition Dec 2025, SBTi CNZS V2 second consultation Nov 2025), and a worked guide to reading a corporate climate disclosure.
- **Chapter 6: Carbon Policy History, 1988–2015.** Full draft, ~10,500 words. Seven parts tracing the institutional pathway from climate science (1979 World Climate Conference, IPCC formation 1988, FAR 1990) through UNFCCC (1992), Kyoto (1997, including the Byrd-Hagel Resolution and US withdrawal in 2001), the CDM and EU ETS launch (2005), Copenhagen's collapse (2009), Cancún and Durban rebuilding (2010–2011), to Paris (2015). Explicit treatment of the Annex I / non-Annex I "firewall" and its political dissolution.
- **Chapter 4: Fossil Fuels and the Carbon Stock We're Burning.** Full draft, ~9,500 words. Six parts covering: what coal/oil/gas physically are; the global oil market (OPEC+, IOCs, NOCs, shale); the regional gas markets plus LNG bridge plus methane leakage; the coal market and China-dominance dynamic; the reserves vs. carbon-budget mismatch and stranded-asset thesis; and a critical reading of the IEA's 2025 World Energy Outlook including the CPS reintroduction.
- **Chapter 3: The CO₂ Molecule Value Chain.** Full draft covering the physical infrastructure for moving CO₂ — sources, capture-and-storage vs. merchant-CO₂ value chains, US CO₂-EOR legacy (~5,000 miles of pipeline, ~70 Mt/yr), the 45Q/IRA/OBBBA economic shifts, the Satartia pipeline rupture and PHMSA's January 2025 NPRM, and the 2025–2030 hub landscape (North Sea, US Gulf Coast, Alberta, Midwest ethanol corridor).
- **Chapter 2: Solid Carbon as Commodity.** Full draft covering metallurgical/petroleum coke, carbon black, activated carbon, natural and synthetic graphite, carbon fiber, and the smaller commodity-carbon markets. Includes the China graphite export-licensing story and the IRA/CBAM response context.
- Glossary expanded with ~30 Chapter 2 entries, Chapter 3 entries, ~25 Chapter 4 entries, ~30 Chapter 5 entries, ~30 Chapter 6 entries, ~25 Chapter 7 entries, and ~30 Chapter 8 entries.

These figures will need updating as new GCB releases (typically November/COP each year) and IPCC assessments are published.
