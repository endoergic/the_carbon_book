# Sourcing Plan — Per Chapter

**Purpose:** For each chapter, the canonical sources I'll draw from when authoring. Mix of (a) **anchor texts** I already know well from training, (b) **regulatory/primary documents** to search and fetch live, (c) **datasets** for quantitative claims, and (d) **journalism/secondary** for narrative and reckoning moments.

**Live-search rule:** Anything price-, policy-, or regulation-current (e.g., current EUA price, latest CBAM implementing regulation, most recent SBTi guidance) gets searched at write-time, not pulled from memory. Same for any post-2024 development.

## Sourcing principles I'll apply consistently

1. **Primary documents over secondary summaries.** If there's a regulation, I cite the regulation, not a press release about it.
2. **Numbers get sourced specifically.** Any number in a chapter has a traceable source, ideally a dataset or primary document.
3. **Acknowledged uncertainty.** Where the literature genuinely disagrees (e.g., DAC cost trajectories, REDD+ baseline methodology, social cost of carbon discount rate), I present the disagreement rather than pretending consensus.
4. **Recency check before each chapter.** Before authoring, I scan for major developments since my training cutoff using web search, particularly for: prices, regulations, court decisions, methodology updates, project status.
5. **Where I can't verify, I flag.** "This figure is from a paywalled industry report; here's the secondary source I'm relying on, treat with appropriate caution."
