# Contributing

Thank you for considering a contribution to *The Carbon Book*.

This is a living educational resource. The most valuable contributions are usually:

1. **Factual corrections** — A number is wrong, a date is wrong, a regulation has been amended, a project has been cancelled or completed.
2. **Source upgrades** — Replacing a secondary citation with the primary document, or pointing to a better-supported figure.
3. **Currency updates** — Atmospheric CO₂, carbon prices, regulatory status, project lists, etc., as time passes.
4. **Clarifications** — Sections that are unclear, ambiguous, or assume too much context.
5. **Filling gaps** — Important topics the book underweights or misses.

## How to suggest a correction

**For small corrections:** Open an issue. Include:
- The chapter and section
- The specific claim you believe is incorrect
- What you believe is correct, and a source for it (a primary document is best)

**For substantive edits:** Open a pull request. Keep PRs focused — one issue per PR is easier to review.

## What's in scope

- Corrections to factual claims, citations, and figures
- Updates to current numbers (prices, concentrations, regulatory status)
- Improvements to clarity and exposition
- Better visuals or diagrams (where these aid understanding)
- Expanding the glossary
- Suggesting new primary sources for the sourcing plan

## What's out of scope (at least for now)

- **Wholesale rewrites of chapters** — this is a single-author work in style, and consistency matters. If you have strong views on framing, open an issue first to discuss.
- **Advocacy content** — the book aims to present contested topics evenhandedly. Contributions that advocate for one side of a contested empirical or political question, rather than improving accuracy, are unlikely to be merged.
- **Translations** — welcome in principle, but as forks/derivative works rather than sub-directories here, at least until the English version is stable.

## On tone

The book tries to be:
- **Direct, not deferential.** It assumes the reader is intelligent and busy.
- **Specific about uncertainty.** Where the literature genuinely disagrees, it says so.
- **Calibrated.** Strong claims need strong evidence; weak ones are framed as such.
- **Free of advocacy.** Even on topics the authors have views on, the framing tries to give the reader space to disagree.

Contributions that match this tone are more likely to be merged with minimal editing.

## On AI-assisted authorship

The book is being written with substantial assistance from a large language model (Claude). Contributors are welcome to use AI tools themselves to draft or polish contributions, but the contributor is responsible for verifying every factual claim in their submission against primary sources. PRs that introduce hallucinated citations, fabricated statistics, or attributions to the wrong source will be rejected, regardless of whether they originated with a human or an AI.

## Licensing of contributions

By submitting a contribution, you agree that your contribution will be licensed under the same terms as the book itself — CC BY 4.0 — and that you have the right to make it under those terms.
