# Disclaimers

## What this book is

*The Carbon Book* is an educational resource. It is intended for readers who want to develop a working understanding of how carbon functions — chemically, industrially, financially, and politically — in the modern world. It is written for a sophisticated lay audience: people with background in adjacent fields who want comprehensive coverage without an undergraduate textbook ramp.

## What this book is not

**This book is not a substitute for professional advice.** Nothing in it constitutes:

- **Legal advice.** Discussion of regulations (UNFCCC, EU ETS, CBAM, IRA §45Q, SBTi guidance, CSRD, SEC climate rules, etc.) is descriptive and educational. Regulations change. If you have a compliance question, retain counsel.
- **Financial or investment advice.** Discussion of carbon prices, carbon project economics, stranded asset risk, climate-related financial disclosure, or any specific company, project, or asset is educational and analytical. It is not a recommendation to buy, sell, hold, or trade anything.
- **Investment due diligence.** Where the book names specific companies, projects, or technologies (e.g., Stegra, LanzaTech, Climeworks, Northern Lights, specific carbon credit projects), this is illustrative of the landscape, not endorsement. Pre-revenue and early-stage climate companies carry substantial risk; many will fail.
- **Engineering or scientific consultation.** The book discusses how technologies work at a conceptual level. Designing, permitting, or operating actual systems requires qualified engineers and scientists.

## Accuracy and currency

Climate, carbon markets, and energy policy move quickly. The book references many time-sensitive figures: atmospheric CO₂ concentration, annual emissions, allowance prices, regulatory status, technology costs, project milestones.

Where the book cites a specific number, it tries to indicate the source and the as-of date. Even so:

- **Some numbers are wrong by the time you read them.** Carbon prices, regulatory milestones, and corporate commitments change. Treat anything market-current as a snapshot.
- **Some numbers were never exactly right.** Estimates of soil carbon stocks, deforestation emissions, methane leakage rates, REDD+ project counterfactuals, social cost of carbon — these involve genuine scientific uncertainty, sometimes by factor-of-two margins. The book tries to flag where the uncertainty is large.
- **The book reflects the views and interpretation of its authors at the time of writing.** Where the literature is contested (e.g., on offset integrity, on DAC cost trajectories, on the timing of stranded-asset risk), the book aims to present the controversy rather than impose a verdict — but the framing itself reflects judgment.

When using the book for any consequential purpose, verify with the underlying source. The `00_SOURCING_PLAN.md` file lists, per chapter, the primary documents and datasets the chapter was built from.

## AI-assisted authorship

This book is being written with substantial assistance from Claude, a large language model produced by Anthropic. The student/editor — the repo owner — is responsible for reviewing the content, fact-checking against primary sources, and publishing the result.

Readers should be aware that:

- **LLM-generated text can contain factual errors, including plausible-sounding but incorrect citations, dates, statistics, and attributions.** The authors attempt to verify and to flag where numbers came from a live web search vs. model recall, but mistakes will be present.
- **Some content reflects information that may be out of date** relative to the LLM's training data cutoff. The book mitigates this by using live web search for current figures and noting the as-of date, but this isn't perfect.
- **Where the book makes interpretive or evaluative claims** — about which technologies will scale, which policies will succeed, which markets are credible — these reflect a particular synthesis and should not be treated as authoritative consensus.

If you find an error, please open an issue or pull request. See `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

## On the framing of contested topics

Several topics covered in this book are contested — not just empirically, but ideologically and politically. Examples include:

- The role of CCUS in a credible energy transition
- The integrity of voluntary carbon markets
- The appropriate role of offsets in corporate net-zero claims
- The economics and ethics of CDR scaling
- The viability of geoengineering research
- The fairness of CBAM and similar border adjustments
- Whether continued fossil fuel investment is consistent with Paris-aligned pathways

The book tries to present these as the live debates they are, with the strongest version of each side, rather than as settled questions. Readers may legitimately come to different conclusions than any framing the book uses. Where the authors have a view, they try to flag it as a view rather than a fact.
