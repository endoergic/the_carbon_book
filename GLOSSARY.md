# Glossary — Carbon Curriculum

Living document. Each entry shows: **term** — definition, plus *(first introduced: Ch. X)* once we've covered it. Seeded with foundational terms; new entries added each session.

---

## Units & basic quantities

- **CO₂** — Carbon dioxide. The specific molecule. One ton of CO₂ contains 12/44 ≈ 0.273 tons of elemental carbon.
- **CO₂e (carbon dioxide equivalent)** — Accounting unit that converts other greenhouse gases into a CO₂-equivalent basis using global warming potential (GWP). One ton of methane ≈ 28 tons CO₂e (GWP-100, AR6).
- **GHG (greenhouse gas)** — The basket of gases regulated under the UNFCCC: CO₂, CH₄ (methane), N₂O (nitrous oxide), HFCs, PFCs, SF₆, NF₃. Note: water vapor is the dominant natural GHG but isn't directly regulated because human emissions don't meaningfully alter atmospheric concentration.
- **GWP (global warming potential)** — Radiative forcing of a gas relative to CO₂ over a specified time horizon, usually 100 years (GWP-100). GWP-20 is sometimes used for methane to highlight short-term impact.
- **ppm (parts per million)** — Concentration unit for atmospheric CO₂. Preindustrial ≈ 280 ppm; current ≈ 425 ppm (Mauna Loa, recent).
- **MtCO₂ / GtCO₂** — Million / billion (giga) tons of CO₂. Global annual fossil emissions ≈ 37 GtCO₂; all anthropogenic GHG ≈ 53 GtCO₂e.
- **tCO₂e** — Metric ton of CO₂-equivalent. The currency unit of carbon markets.

## Core conceptual terms

- **Carbon cycle** — The set of natural fluxes that move carbon among atmosphere, ocean, terrestrial biosphere, and geological reservoirs. "Fast" cycle = biosphere/ocean exchanges on years-to-centuries. "Slow" cycle = geological exchange on millennia-plus.
- **Carbon budget** — Cumulative future CO₂ emissions consistent with a given probability of staying below a temperature threshold (e.g., remaining 1.5°C budget at 50% probability ≈ 200–300 GtCO₂ as of recent IPCC estimates — search for current figure).
- **Net zero** — A state in which residual anthropogenic GHG emissions are balanced by anthropogenic removals over a defined period. Not the same as zero emissions. The definition (which gases, which removals count, what timeframe) is contested.
- **Net negative** — Removals exceed emissions over a period. Required globally in most 1.5°C-compatible pathways for the second half of the century.

## Accounting & corporate

- **Scope 1** — Direct emissions from sources owned or controlled by the reporting entity.
- **Scope 2** — Indirect emissions from purchased electricity, steam, heat, cooling.
- **Scope 3** — All other indirect emissions in the value chain (upstream and downstream). 15 categories under GHG Protocol.
- **GHG Protocol** — The dominant corporate accounting framework, jointly developed by WRI and WBCSD. Corporate Standard (2004), Scope 3 Standard (2011), Land Sector and Removals Guidance (2024).
- **MRV (Measurement, Reporting, Verification)** — The umbrella term for the process by which emissions, reductions, or removals are quantified, documented, and independently confirmed. The credibility of every market and disclosure regime depends on MRV quality.

## Market structures

- **Compliance market** — Carbon market where participation is mandated by law. EU ETS, California CCA, RGGI, China national ETS, etc.
- **Voluntary carbon market (VCM)** — Market in which buyers purchase credits without regulatory obligation, typically to meet self-set climate commitments or for product claims.
- **Cap and trade** — Quantity-based instrument: regulator sets a cap on total emissions, allocates or auctions allowances, allows trading. EUA, CCA, RGA are the allowance types in the major systems.
- **Carbon tax** — Price-based instrument: regulator sets a price per ton, quantity adjusts. Sweden, BC, Switzerland are flagship examples.
- **Carbon credit / offset** — A tradable unit representing one tCO₂e of avoided, reduced, or removed emissions, issued by a standard/registry against a verified project.

## Chapter 1 additions (introduced: Ch. 1)

- **Airborne fraction** — The proportion of annual anthropogenic CO₂ emissions that remains in the atmosphere (rather than being absorbed by ocean or land sinks). Historically about 45% on average; may rise as sinks weaken.
- **Allotrope** — Different structural forms of the same chemical element. Carbon's allotropes include diamond, graphite, graphene, amorphous carbon, fullerenes, and nanotubes.
- **Climate sensitivity** — Long-term global mean surface temperature change per doubling of atmospheric CO₂.
  - **ECS (Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity)** — The full equilibrium response. AR6 "likely" range: 2.5–4.0°C.
  - **TCR (Transient Climate Response)** — The response at the time of doubling under 1%/yr increase. Smaller than ECS.
- **GPP (Gross Primary Productivity)** — Total photosynthetic carbon fixation by terrestrial plants. ~120 GtC/yr globally.
- **GWP-20 / GWP-100** — Global Warming Potential integrated over 20 or 100 years. GWP-100 is the regulatory standard; GWP-20 emphasizes short-lived gases like methane.
- **GWP\*** — Alternative metric (Allen et al.) that treats short-lived gases like methane differently than standard GWP. Not yet widely adopted in regulation.
- **Net negative emissions** — A state in which gross anthropogenic removals exceed gross anthropogenic emissions. Required globally in most 1.5°C-compatible IPCC pathways for the second half of the century.
- **PgC / GtC** — Petagrams / gigatons of carbon (equivalent units). Atmospheric science often uses GtC; policy and markets use GtCO₂. 1 GtC = 3.67 GtCO₂.
- **Radiative forcing (RF)** — The change in net downward radiative flux at the tropopause from a perturbation. Measured in W/m². Anthropogenic GHG RF since 1750 ≈ +3.2 W/m².
- **Stranded asset** — An asset that loses economic value before the end of its expected useful life due to changing market or regulatory conditions. In climate context: fossil fuel reserves or infrastructure that cannot be exploited under emissions constraints.
- **Suess effect** — The observed decrease in the atmospheric ¹³C/¹²C ratio due to addition of isotopically light fossil-fuel carbon. Named after Hans Suess (1955). Key isotopic evidence that the modern CO₂ rise is anthropogenic.
- **TCRE (Transient Climate Response to cumulative Emissions)** — The near-linear relationship between cumulative CO₂ emissions and peak warming. Central estimate ~0.45°C per 1,000 GtCO₂.
- **Unburnable carbon** — The fraction of proved fossil fuel reserves whose CO₂ emissions would exceed the remaining carbon budget for a chosen temperature target. Carbon Tracker, 2011.

### Refined / re-anchored entries from Chapter 1

- **Anthropogenic** — Caused by human activity. From Greek *anthrōpos* (human) + *-genic* (producing). The standard opposing term to *natural* in climate science. Climate policy concerns only the anthropogenic share of emissions and removals; natural fluxes are not subject to regulation.
- **Anthropogenic perturbation** — The human-caused disturbance of an otherwise quasi-balanced natural system. The carbon cycle was approximately in balance before industrialization (gross fluxes in ≈ gross fluxes out); anthropogenic emissions are the one-directional addition that perturbs the balance. The "perturbation" framing forces analysis in terms of *changes from baseline* rather than absolute flows.
- **Atomic structure (brief)** — An atom consists of (a) a **nucleus** containing **protons** (positively charged) and **neutrons** (uncharged), and (b) **electrons** (negatively charged) arranged in shells around the nucleus. Carbon has 6 protons + 6 neutrons in the nucleus, and 6 electrons in two shells: 2 in the inner shell (filled), 4 in the outer shell (half-filled, can hold 8). **Only outer-shell electrons participate in chemical bonding.** This is why carbon forms four bonds: four outer-shell electrons available to share.
- **Buffer capacity (ocean)** — The ocean's ability to absorb additional CO₂ declines as it acidifies. The chemistry (carbonate ↔ bicarbonate ↔ dissolved CO₂ equilibria) becomes progressively less favorable as more CO₂ is dissolved. This is the chemistry-limit on ocean CO₂ uptake, separate from the kinetic-limit (slow surface-to-deep mixing).
- **Carbon budget** — Now anchored: remaining 1.5°C budget ~170 GtCO₂ (50% probability); 1.7°C ~525 GtCO₂; 2°C ~1,055 GtCO₂ (GCB 2025).
- **Clausius-Clapeyron relation** — Physical law governing how much water vapor air can hold at a given temperature. Warmer air holds ~7% more water vapor per °C. The reason water vapor is a feedback (not a forcing) on climate change: anthropogenic water-vapor emissions can't persistently change atmospheric concentration because the temperature governs it.
- **CO₂e (carbon dioxide equivalent)** — Now anchored to AR6 GWP-100 values: CH₄ ~30, N₂O ~273, HFC-134a ~1,530, SF₆ ~25,200.
- **Delocalized electron** — An electron that is not confined to a single bond between two specific atoms, but instead spread across multiple atoms in a molecular orbital. In graphite, each carbon contributes one delocalized electron to a sheet-wide π-orbital system, which is what makes graphite electrically conductive along the sheet plane. (Diamond has no delocalized electrons — all four of each carbon's outer electrons are tied up in tetrahedral bonds — which is why diamond is an insulator.)
- **Feedback (climate)** — A process in the climate system that amplifies (positive) or dampens (negative) the response to a forcing. Water vapor is the largest positive feedback to CO₂ forcing: CO₂ warms → air holds more water vapor → water vapor amplifies warming → repeat. Roughly doubles the climate sensitivity over what CO₂ alone would produce.
- **Forcing (radiative)** — An imposed change in the Earth's radiation budget (W/m²), distinct from feedbacks which are the system's response to a forcing. Anthropogenic CO₂ is a forcing; water vapor responding to that CO₂-driven warming is a feedback.
- **GCB / Global Carbon Budget (2025 edition)** — Annual scientific assessment by the Global Carbon Project (>100 scientists) tracking all major flows of the global carbon cycle. Published each November in conjunction with COP, in *Earth System Science Data*. The 2025 edition (Friedlingstein et al., Nov 13, 2025) is the canonical reference for current carbon-cycle quantities used in this curriculum. Headline 2025 findings: ocean sink revised up to 29% of cumulative emissions, land sink revised down to 21%, remaining 1.5°C budget ~170 GtCO₂ ("virtually exhausted").
- **Isotopic composition** — The relative proportions of an element's different isotopes in a sample. For carbon: the proportions of ¹²C (~98.9%), ¹³C (~1.1%), and ¹⁴C (trace). Two CO₂ samples can be chemically identical but isotopically different — one from a volcano, one from burning coal — distinguishable by isotope ratio mass spectrometry. The basis for the Suess effect, radiocarbon dating, and biogenic vs. fossil carbon verification in fuel and product certification.
- **Molecular weight** — Mass of one mole of a substance's molecules (g/mol). CO₂ = 44 (12 C + 16+16 O); CH₄ = 16; H₂O = 18. Distinct from density (mass per volume). The 12-vs-44 molecular-weight difference is why **1 tCO₂ = 0.273 tC** and **1 tC = 3.67 tCO₂**.
- **ppm (parts per million)** — Anchored: 2025 projected annual mean ≈ 425.7 ppm; preindustrial baseline ≈ 278 ppm.

## Chapter 2 additions (introduced: Ch. 2)

- **Anode (battery)** — The electrode in a lithium-ion cell into which lithium ions intercalate during charging. Almost always graphite or graphite-blend in current commercial cells.
- **Anode-grade petcoke** — Low-sulfur, low-metals petroleum coke used as a precursor for aluminum smelter anodes and some synthetic graphite. Distinct from fuel-grade petcoke.
- **Battery passport** — EU regulatory regime (effective 2027) requiring cradle-to-gate carbon footprint and material disclosure for batteries above 2 kWh sold in the EU.
- **BF-BOF (Blast Furnace–Basic Oxygen Furnace)** — The dominant primary steelmaking route globally. Coke-intensive; ~1.8–2.3 tCO₂ per ton of steel.
- **Blast furnace** — Tall reactor (30+ m) in which iron ore, coke, and limestone are charged from the top and reduced to molten iron by hot gas blast from below.
- **Carbon black** — Engineered amorphous carbon particulate, produced primarily by partial combustion of heavy petroleum oils. ~14 Mt/yr global production. Largest use: tire reinforcement (~66% of demand).
- **Carbon fiber / CFRP (Carbon Fiber Reinforced Polymer)** — Manufactured carbon filaments, almost always combined with a polymer matrix (epoxy) to form composite structures. Aerospace, wind blades, hydrogen storage applications. ~150 kt/yr global production.
- **CFRP** — See Carbon fiber.
- **Coke (metallurgical / "met coke")** — Carbon-rich solid produced by destructive distillation of coking coal in the absence of air. Reducing agent and structural support in blast furnaces. ~700–800 Mt/yr global production; the largest carbon commodity by mass.
- **Coking coal** — Premium bituminous coal with specific properties (volatile content, swelling behavior) needed for coke production. ~755 Mt/yr global consumption in steelmaking (2022).
- **CSPG (Coated Spherical Purified Graphite)** — Battery-anode-grade natural graphite after spheronization, acid purification (typically HF), and pyrolytic carbon coating. The form that actually goes into a battery.
- **EAF (Electric Arc Furnace)** — Alternative steelmaking route using electricity to melt scrap or DRI. Lower CO₂ emissions than BF-BOF if powered by clean electricity. Consumes synthetic graphite electrodes.
- **FEOC (Foreign Entity of Concern)** — US regulatory category under the IRA disqualifying battery materials from "foreign entities of concern" (operationally, China and Russia) from EV tax credit eligibility. Graphite-specific compliance has been extended due to feasibility.
- **Flake graphite** — Natural graphite in large-crystal form, the variant most suitable for battery anode processing. Distinct from amorphous and vein graphite.
- **Furnace black** — Dominant carbon black production process: partial combustion of carbon black oil in a controlled furnace.
- **Graphitization** — Thermal process at 2,500–3,000°C that converts disordered carbon precursors (typically needle coke or pitch) into ordered synthetic graphite. Highly energy-intensive (~15,000–20,000 kWh/ton).
- **Hall-Héroult process** — Conventional electrolytic aluminum smelting process. Consumes ~0.4 t of carbon anode per ton of aluminum produced.
- **Hard carbon** — Disordered amorphous carbon used as the anode material in sodium-ion batteries. Related to but distinct from activated carbon.
- **MATS (Mercury and Air Toxics Standards)** — US EPA rule, finalized 2012, requiring mercury and HAP emission controls at coal-fired power plants. Drove the powdered activated carbon market in the 2010s.
- **Needle coke** — Premium petroleum coke or coal-tar pitch product used as the precursor for synthetic graphite electrodes and battery anodes. Strategic upstream material.
- **PAC (Powdered Activated Carbon)** — Powder-form activated carbon, used in flue-gas injection and certain water-treatment applications.
- **PAN (Polyacrylonitrile)** — The dominant precursor polymer for high-performance carbon fiber.
- **PCI (Pulverized Coal Injection)** — Injection of pulverized coal directly into blast furnace tuyeres to reduce coke consumption. Cost-saving measure with limits.
- **Petroleum coke (petcoke)** — Carbon residue from delayed coker units in petroleum refining. ~150 Mt/yr global production. Two grades: anode (calcined) and fuel.
- **rCB (recovered carbon black)** — Carbon black recovered by pyrolysis of end-of-life tires. Emerging circular-economy product; capacity projected to exceed 1 Mt/yr by 2030.
- **Spherical graphite** — Natural graphite that has been micronized and mechanically rounded to ~10–25 µm spherical particles, the form required for battery anode use.
- **Spheronization** — Mechanical process of rounding angular flake graphite into spherical particles for battery anode use.
- **Synthetic graphite** — Manufactured graphite produced by high-temperature graphitization of needle coke or pitch precursors. Higher cost and energy intensity than natural graphite, but more consistent properties.

## Chapter 3 additions (introduced: Ch. 3)

- **45Q tax credit** — Section 45Q of the US Internal Revenue Code, providing a per-ton tax credit for CO₂ captured and either sequestered or used. Created 2008, expanded by FUTURE Act (2018) and Inflation Reduction Act (2022), modified by OBBBA (2025) to provide parity between sequestration and EOR. Current values: $85/tCO₂ for point-source sequestration, $180/tCO₂ for DAC sequestration; EOR/utilization now matches post-OBBBA.
- **ACTL (Alberta Carbon Trunk Line)** — Operating Canadian CO₂ pipeline (~240 km), connecting a fertilizer plant and refinery to EOR operations. ~1.6 Mt/yr capacity, operational since 2020.
- **Aramis** — Proposed Dutch CCS hub targeting up to 22 Mt/yr long-term capacity. Pre-FID as of 2026.
- **Acorn** — UK Track-1 CCUS cluster in Scotland, approved October 2023.
- **Bravo Dome / McElmo Dome / Sheep Mountain / Jackson Dome** — Naturally-occurring CO₂ reservoirs in the western US, "mined" to supply CO₂-EOR operations. Source of much historical US CO₂-EOR CO₂.
- **CCU (Carbon Capture and Utilization)** — Capturing CO₂ for use in products (fuels, chemicals, materials, mineralization) rather than permanent storage. Distinct from CCS.
- **CCUS** — Umbrella term covering both CCS (storage) and CCU (utilization).
- **CO₂-EOR (Enhanced Oil Recovery)** — Injection of CO₂ into oil reservoirs to mobilize remaining oil. ~70 Mt CO₂/yr injected in the US, the largest historical use of large-scale CO₂ infrastructure.
- **Class VI well** — US EPA-regulated well class specifically for permanent CO₂ storage. Distinct from Class II (EOR) and Class I (industrial waste). Slow EPA permitting; some states (LA, ND, WY) have obtained primacy.
- **Critical point (CO₂)** — The thermodynamic state above which CO₂ exists as a supercritical fluid: 31.1°C and 73.8 bar.
- **Denbury / Denbury Gulf Coast Pipelines** — Historically the largest US CO₂ pipeline network operator. Acquired by ExxonMobil in 2023 for $4.9 billion. Operator at the time of the 2020 Satartia rupture.
- **EOR (Enhanced Oil Recovery)** — General term for techniques to extract additional oil from a reservoir beyond primary and secondary recovery. CO₂-EOR is one approach.
- **LCOA (Levelized Cost of CO₂ Avoided)** — Cost per ton of net CO₂ avoided by a capture project, accounting for parasitic emissions of capture itself. Higher than LCOC.
- **LCOC (Levelized Cost of CO₂ Captured)** — Cost per ton of CO₂ captured, before accounting for parasitic emissions.
- **Longship** — Norwegian government's full-scale CCS program, which includes Northern Lights. Government covers ~80% of Phase 1 costs.
- **Merchant CO₂** — Industrial-grade CO₂ sold as a commodity to thousands of end customers (beverages, food, welding, greenhouses, water treatment, etc.). ~$13 billion global market 2024.
- **MMV (Measurement, Monitoring, and Verification)** — The framework for confirming permanent storage of injected CO₂. Class VI wells require detailed MMV plans for 50-year post-injection periods.
- **Navigator CO2 Ventures** — Cancelled (October 2023) Midwest CO₂ pipeline project, an instructive failure case study in CCS pipeline politics.
- **Northern Lights** — World's first open-access (third-party) CO₂ transport and storage facility, operational since August 2025. JV of Equinor, Shell, TotalEnergies. Phase 1: 1.5 Mt/yr; Phase 2: 5+ Mt/yr by 2028.
- **NPRM (Notice of Proposed Rulemaking)** — US administrative law step in which an agency publishes a proposed regulation for public comment before issuing a final rule.
- **OBBBA (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, July 2025)** — US reconciliation legislation that established 45Q parity between sequestration and EOR/utilization, and introduced Foreign Entity of Concern restrictions to several clean energy tax credits.
- **Pathways Alliance** — Consortium of six Canadian oil sands operators proposing ~10 Mt/yr CCS for oil sands. Pre-FID as of 2026; politically contested.
- **PHMSA (Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration)** — US DOT agency responsible for pipeline safety regulation. Issued January 2025 NPRM updating CO₂ pipeline safety rules in response to Satartia.
- **Pipeline phase (CO₂)** — Thermodynamic state of CO₂ in transport: supercritical (most common for long-distance), liquid (trucking/shipping), or gas (short-distance, lower-pressure applications).
- **Porthos** — Dutch CCS hub project, Rotterdam-area sources to North Sea storage. ~2.5 Mt/yr, first injection expected ~2026.
- **Saline aquifer / saline formation** — Deep underground geological formation containing saltwater, suitable for CO₂ storage. The favored target for most large-scale CCS.
- **Satartia, Mississippi (Feb 22, 2020)** — Town near the rupture of a Denbury CO₂ pipeline. >45 hospitalizations, no fatalities. The defining US CO₂ pipeline safety incident; basis for PHMSA's January 2025 NPRM.
- **Shute Creek / LaBarge** — ExxonMobil's Wyoming gas processing facility; the world's largest single anthropogenic CO₂ source for EOR (~7 Mt/yr captured).
- **Stratos** — Occidental/1PointFive's Permian Basin DAC facility (first phase 500,000 t/yr capture from atmosphere, commercial operation 2025).
- **Summit Carbon Solutions** — Largest proposed Midwest CO₂ pipeline project. Substantially restructured 2025–2026 from a 5-state Dakota-storage plan to a Nebraska-Wyoming routing, including stated interest in EOR uses post-OBBBA.
- **Supercritical CO₂** — CO₂ above its critical point. Dense, single-phase fluid; the typical state for long-distance pipeline transport.

## Chapter 4 additions (introduced: Ch. 4)

- **API gravity** — Measure of crude oil density; higher API = lighter crude. Light sweet crude commands premium prices.
- **Bituminous coal** — The dominant coal grade globally (60–90% C). Used for power generation and (when low in ash/sulfur) coking coal for steelmaking.
- **CNG (Compressed Natural Gas)** — Natural gas at 200–250 bar pressure, used as vehicle fuel in some markets.
- **CPS (Current Policies Scenario)** — IEA scenario reintroduced in WEO 2025 after a 5-year hiatus, under US pressure. Assumes governments fail to implement announced policies. Oil demand grows to 113 mb/d by 2050; implies ~2.9°C warming.
- **Crude oil benchmarks** — Brent (North Sea light sweet), WTI (Texas light sweet), Dubai/Oman (Middle East medium), Bonny Light (Nigeria), Maya (Mexico heavy sour). Most globally-traded crudes price at spreads to these.
- **Henry Hub** — US natural gas price benchmark (Louisiana). Sets pricing for most North American gas.
- **IOC (International Oil Company)** — Publicly-traded supermajor: ExxonMobil, Chevron, Shell, TotalEnergies, BP, Eni, Equinor. Combined upstream production ~11 mb/d.
- **JKM (Japan-Korea Marker)** — Asian LNG benchmark, set by spot LNG cargoes delivered to Japan and Korea.
- **Lignite (brown coal)** — Lowest-energy coal grade (25–35% C). Almost always burned at the mine-mouth because not economic to transport.
- **LNG (Liquefied Natural Gas)** — Natural gas cooled to ~–162°C (1/600 the volume of gas), shipped on specialized tankers. Total global trade ~410 Mt (560 bcm equivalent) in 2024.
- **Methane leakage** — Loss of unburned methane during natural gas production, processing, transport, and use. Recent satellite measurements: 2–4% leakage from major producing basins, substantially higher than industry self-reports. At >3% leakage, gas loses its climate advantage over coal on a 20-year basis.
- **NGLs (Natural Gas Liquids)** — Heavier hydrocarbon components (ethane, propane, butane) separated from natural gas. Sold as petrochemical feedstock or LPG.
- **NOC (National Oil Company)** — State-owned oil company. Saudi Aramco, ADNOC, Pemex, Petrobras, CNPC, ONGC, Rosneft, and others. Together control most global reserves.
- **NZE (Net Zero Emissions by 2050)** — IEA's normative scenario consistent with 1.5°C (with overshoot in 2025 version). Requires no new fossil fuel field development after 2025.
- **OPEC+ / OPEC** — Producer cartel coordinating oil supply decisions. OPEC = 13 countries; OPEC+ adds Russia and others, controls ~41% of global oil production.
- **R/P ratio (Reserves-to-Production)** — Years that proved reserves would last at current production rates. Oil: ~50; Gas: ~47; Coal: ~118.
- **Shale revolution** — US tight oil and gas production growth via horizontal drilling + hydraulic fracturing, ~2005–2020. Made the US a net liquids exporter ~2020 and the world's largest LNG exporter ~2022.
- **Sour / sweet crude** — Sour = high sulfur (>0.5%); sweet = low sulfur (<0.5%).
- **STEPS (Stated Policies Scenario)** — IEA scenario based on currently-stated government policies and announced plans. Oil peaks ~2030, gas peaks ~2035, coal peaks before 2030. Implies ~2.5°C warming.
- **Strait of Hormuz** — Strategic chokepoint between Iran and Oman through which ~20 mb/d of crude oil transit. Single most consequential energy chokepoint globally.
- **Tight oil / shale oil** — Oil produced from low-permeability shale formations using horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing. US tight oil ~13 mb/d in 2024.
- **TTF (Title Transfer Facility)** — European natural gas price benchmark (Netherlands).
- **WEO (World Energy Outlook)** — Annual IEA flagship report; canonical global reference for energy demand projections. 2025 edition (published Nov 2025 at COP30) reintroduced CPS scenario under US pressure.
