# Appendix to Chapters 7 & 8 — Where Fuel-Based VCM White Space Actually Exists

*A cross-geography stacking analysis of renewable-fuel project economics, and the narrow band where voluntary carbon credits remain defensible on top of compliance revenue.*

*Prices and program parameters grounded to Q1–Q2 2026. All figures are illustrative and directional — credit prices are volatile and program rules are revised frequently. Do not use as a financial model without current verification.*

---

## 0. The one idea this appendix is built on

A fuel's emissions reduction can be monetized in two structurally different ways:

1. **Compliance / fuel-standard credits** (RIN, LCFS, 45Z, ReFuelEU, FuelEU Maritime). These use a **statutory baseline** — no counterfactual additionality test. This is where the overwhelming majority of fuel-decarbonization money is.
2. **Voluntary carbon credits (VCM)** — require proving a **counterfactual** (additionality) and that the attribute isn't already claimed (no double-counting).

The central, counterintuitive result — **the additionality inversion** — governs everything below:

> The more compliance revenue a project earns, the *less* room there is for a defensible VCM credit on top, because the compliance stack has already (a) caused the reduction and (b) claimed the attribute.

So "white space" for fuel VCMs is **not** found where the economics are best. It is found in the three places compliance revenue *doesn't reach*:

- **Geographic gap** — no compliance regime exists (bridge-financing window).
- **Boundary gap** — a real reduction the regime's accounting methodology structurally cannot score.
- **Pre-FID swing** — the project doesn't clear its hurdle on compliance alone, and the VCM credit is the decisive, contracted, *additional* dollar at FID.

Everything that looks like white space but isn't (bankable revamps, feedstock-CI gains, generic Scope 2 the regime already credits) is a **double-counting / non-additionality trap.**

## 1. The regimes, by the only question that matters: *what's the baseline?*

| Regime | Geography | Unit | Baseline type | Rewards continuous improvement? | Additionality test? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RFS / RIN** | US federal | RIN (per gal-equiv) | Volumetric step (pass/fail threshold) | **No** — over-performance earns nothing | No (statutory) |
| **LCFS** | CA, OR, WA, NM (+) | Credit (tCO2e) | Distance below declining CI benchmark | **Yes** — credits scale with CI gap, re-certified | No (statutory) |
| **45Z** | US federal | Tax credit ($/gal) | CI-scaled (up to ~$1.00/gal SAF) | Partly — value scales with CI | No (statutory) |
| **REDIII** | EU (national transposition) | Varies by member state | Mostly default CI by feedstock | **Weakly** — default values blunt plant-level gains | No (statutory) |
| **ReFuelEU Aviation** | EU airports | Blending obligation | Volume mandate (step-like) | **No** — rewards qualifying, not improvement | No (statutory) |
| **FuelEU Maritime** | EU port calls | GHG-intensity target | Distance below declining target; RFNBO 2x to 2033 | Yes, but regime immature | No (statutory) |
| **CORSIA** | Intl aviation | Offset/SAF eligibility | Baseline + eligible-fuel benefit | n/a | Yes (VCM-style) |
| **VCM** | Global | Credit (tCO2e) | **Counterfactual** | n/a | **Yes** |

**Reference prices (Q1–Q2 2026, illustrative):**
- LCFS credit: ~$63/MT Q1 2026 (recovering off a 2025 low near $40; 2024 avg ~$60). Price cap $275.39/MT effective June 2026. Market structurally tightening as the benchmark ratchets and the credit bank draws down.
- 45Z (SAF): up to ~$1.00/gal depending on CI; available through 2029.
- SAF book-and-claim certificate (voluntary): ~$1,500–$3,500 per tCO2e reduced (much pricier than generic offsets; "reduction at the source" premium).
- ReFuelEU: demand is **mandate-driven (volume)**, not improvement-driven — the structural point for the EU white-space thesis.
- FuelEU Maritime: high penalty price is the incentive; RFNBO green ammonia counts **double** toward GHG-intensity targets until 2033.

## 2. The four worked cells

Each cell: the compliance stack, then the **VCM verdict** (where, if anywhere, a defensible voluntary credit exists), then the **trap** to avoid.

### Cell A — HEFA in North America (NAM) & Latin America (LATAM)

**NAM stack (per gallon, illustrative):** fuel premium + D4/D5 RIN + LCFS credit (if sold into CA/OR/WA) + 45Z (up to $1.00). This is a *thick* stack. A US HEFA SAF gallon sold into California can stack RIN + LCFS + 45Z simultaneously.

**VCM verdict (NAM): essentially no white space.** The stack is so rich that projects are bankable on compliance alone → non-additional. The attribute is claimed by the RIN and/or LCFS credit the moment it's generated. A voluntary credit on top is double-claiming.

**LATAM stack:** little or no domestic compliance value. The opportunity is **export** (ship physical HEFA/SAF to the EU to capture ReFuelEU value, or sell RIN-bearing molecules into the US) — but you can only sell each attribute *once*. The cleaner LATAM play is **book-and-claim into a richer market**, carefully avoiding double issuance.

**VCM verdict (LATAM): genuine bridge-window white space.** A LATAM HEFA project with no domestic regime, financed *because* VCM (or pre-purchase SAF certificate) revenue makes it pencil, is the cleanest fuel-VCM case there is — *until* the molecules/attributes reach a compliance market, at which point the attribute is claimed there instead.

**Trap:** the "near-zero-margin refinery that becomes viable once it takes UCO." If the UCO + mandate/premium made it viable, the *mandate* caused the reduction → non-additional. Bankability-on-compliance is evidence *against* additionality.

---

### Cell B — eSAF / RFNBO in the EU

**Stack:** ReFuelEU synthetic sub-mandate (volume) + EU ETS interaction (tank-to-wake SAF exempt) + member-state REDIII value + very high physical price (eSAF is the most expensive pathway). Demand is **mandate-driven volume**, and ReFuelEU does **not** reward continuous operator CI improvement.

**VCM verdict: the boundary-gap white space lives here, and it's the most interesting cell.** Because REDIII leans on **default CI values by feedstock** and ReFuelEU rewards *volume* not *improvement*, real operator-driven reductions that the certified-CI methodology **cannot score** are left unclaimed. The defensible VCM credit is **not** the existence of the eSAF plant (the mandate owns that) — it is the **improvement delta the EU's coarse accounting structurally cannot capture.**

**The decisive screen (the single fact):** *Can the operator move their certified CI score by reporting this improvement (via REDIII default or actual-value pathway)?*
- **Yes** → compliance captures it → **not** VCM-eligible (e.g., switching to a lower-CI feedstock; generic Scope 2 the methodology already counts).
- **No, the methodology has no field for it** → candidate white space (e.g., a waste-heat-recovery or clean-power improvement that REDIII's electricity-accounting rules refuse to credit despite being physically real).

**Trap:** feedstock-CI gains. They are exactly what REDIII *is built to measure*, sit in the most fraud-scrutinized zone (UCO relabeling), and are captured inside compliance. Not white space.

---

### Cell C — Biomethane in NAM & EU

**NAM stack:** the jackpot case under **LCFS** specifically. Dairy/landfill biomethane has **deeply negative CI** (often ~ -250 gCO2e/MJ), so distance below the ~95 benchmark is enormous — a single MJ generates credits as if it displaced several MJ of fossil. LCFS rewards this; RFS (volumetric) cannot see it. Plus D3 RINs + 45Z.

**VCM verdict (NAM): no white space, *and* an integrity warning.** The negative-CI value is fully captured by LCFS — over-captured, arguably. There is an active critique that LCFS *over*-pays manure-gas and may induce perverse incentives (more lagoons to harvest the subsidy). A VCM credit on top is both non-additional and reputationally radioactive.

**EU stack:** biomethane value via REDIII/national schemes; again default-value-leaning, less granular than LCFS.

**VCM verdict (EU): same boundary-gap logic as Cell B** — only the improvement delta the certified score can't capture is a candidate, subject to the same decisive screen. Narrow.

**Teaching note:** the negative-CI mechanic is itself a *counterfactual/avoidance claim smuggled into a fuel score* — structurally the same as a REDD+ avoidance credit, with the same additionality and perverse-incentive vulnerabilities. This is the bridge back to the Chapter 8 integrity framework.

---

### Cell D — eAmmonia for marine (EU / SEA / China)

**Stack:** **FuelEU Maritime** (declining GHG-intensity target; green ammonia / RFNBO counts **2x** to 2033) + **EU ETS** (maritime now included; high penalty/price is the stick) + national green-H2/ammonia subsidies (US clean-H2 credits, EU/Asia programs). SEA (Singapore as the world's largest bunkering hub) and China are building bunkering capacity fast, but **compliance value is EU-anchored** — it accrues on EU port calls.

**VCM verdict: a high-risk bridge window, different in kind from the others.** The regimes are *immature* — IMO's global mechanism is still forming, bunkering standards are 2026–27, engines commercial ~2026. For a green-ammonia project in SEA/China serving non-EU routes, there is **no mature compliance buyer yet**, so VCM/pre-purchase could bridge FID — *but* the additionality story competes with heavy direct subsidies, and the stranding risk is acute: when IMO's mechanism and/or FuelEU coverage arrives, the attribute gets claimed there and the VCM stream dies.

**Trap:** double-counting across the EU-anchored stack (FuelEU 2x multiplier + ETS exemption + a VCM claim on the same tonne). The 2x multiplier in particular is a *compliance accounting* construct, not a license to also sell the tonne voluntarily.

## 3. White-space ranking — *for VCMs*, not for project economics

The question you asked: **which regulated fuel markets have the best VCM white space?** Ranked best→worst for *defensible* voluntary credit opportunity (the inverse of where compliance economics are richest):

| Rank | Where | Type of white space | Why | Half-life / risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deregulated geographies (LATAM, parts of SEA/Africa)** | Geographic / bridge | No regime claims the attribute; VCM is genuinely the financing that makes it additional | **Finite** — dies when local regulation arrives (the "toll booth that closes behind you") |
| 2 | **EU eSAF / biomethane improvement delta** | Boundary gap | REDIII default-value + ReFuelEU volume logic can't score continuous operator CI gains | **Shrinking** — methodology revisions close cracks |
| 3 | **Pre-FID marginal projects (any geography)** | Financial-additionality swing | Project doesn't pencil on compliance alone; contracted VCM is decisive at FID | Compliance-price risk — if LCFS/SAF premium rises post-FID, additionality is retroactively challengeable |
| 4 | **Marine eAmmonia (non-EU routes, near-term)** | Immature-regime bridge | No mature compliance buyer yet for non-EU exposure | **Acute stranding risk** + competes with direct subsidies; double-counting hazard |
| — | **NAM HEFA / NAM biomethane / EU mandate-covered SAF** | **None** | Compliance stack already causes the reduction and claims the attribute | n/a — these are traps |

**Headline:** the **single best VCM white space is the deregulated-geography bridge** (LATAM HEFA being the cleanest worked example), and the **most defensible structural play in regulated markets is the EU improvement-delta** — precisely because the EU rewards volume/qualification rather than continuous improvement. California's LCFS, by being the "best and truest" CI market, leaves almost *no* VCM white space — its granularity is exactly what closes the gap.

## 4. How to actually structure the defensible cases

**The pre-FID swing (Rank 3), done right.** Make the VCM offtake **demonstrably load-bearing at FID**: a signed credit pre-purchase in the financing case the bank's credit committee approves, where the committee memo shows the project does *not* clear without it. That memo doubles as your additionality evidence — the bank's bankability documentation and the VVB's additionality file become *the same document*. Risk: you are underwriting additionality against an *uncertain future compliance price*; rising LCFS/SAF premiums can retroactively undermine the claim.

**The bridge window (Rank 1), done right.** Underwrite the project on a **finite** VCM revenue period followed by a transition to compliance revenue when local regulation arrives — and model the **handoff explicitly** (the government may claim the attribute without compensating you, set a low price, or grandfather you — or not). This handoff model is the appendix's genuinely novel contribution; standard texts omit it.

**The improvement delta (Rank 2), done right.** Diligence the *specific* reductions that fall outside **both** REDIII's default value **and** its actual-value option, that **no one is otherwise claiming**. If the operator *could* report it via actual-value and simply hasn't, it's not a credit — it's unfiled paperwork. The edge is knowing the methodology's blind spots better than the regulator's table does; the edge has a half-life.

## 5. Bridge back to Chapters 7 & 8

- The **negative-CI mechanic** (Cell C) is a counterfactual avoidance claim inside a fuel score — same integrity profile as REDD+ avoidance credits in Ch 8.
- The **permanence asymmetry**: fuel reductions pass the reversal test that sinks forestry, but fail the additionality test forestry can sometimes pass. Different failure modes, same four-test framework.
- The **additionality inversion** is the unifying thesis: durable-removal and compliance-fuel camps both exist *because* avoidance/fuel additionality is hard to defend — which is why Ch 8 (a VCM chapter) has so little to say about fuel offsets. They mostly aren't VCM instruments. This appendix maps the narrow band where they still are.
