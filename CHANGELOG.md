### Added
- **Chapter 9: Carbon Pricing — The Broader View.** Full draft, ~7,300 words. Seven parts: the four distinct meanings of "carbon price" (compliance market, carbon tax, internal corporate, social cost of carbon); the SCC in depth (Biden $190/tCO₂ → Trump 2025 ~$1/tCO₂; IWG history; EO 14154; methodological choices driving the $1–$1,000+ range); carbon taxes globally (Sweden $130, Canada national C$80→C$170, Switzerland $150, Singapore S$25→S$50, BC, France's frozen €44, etc.); internal corporate carbon prices (Microsoft $100 fee vs. shadow prices); CBAM and carbon-club concept (Nordhaus); coverage gap analysis (24% of global emissions priced but only 8–10% at materially high levels); cap-and-trade vs. carbon taxes vs. alternatives synthesis.
- **Chapter 10: CCUS.** Full draft, ~7,600 words. Seven parts: technology stack (post-combustion, pre-combustion, oxy-fuel, DAC); CCUS project value chain (capture → compression → transport → injection → MRV → stewardship); current state (50 Mt/yr Q1 2025; 430 Mt/yr pipeline by 2030; NZE 1,000 Mt/yr needed; 60% of operational capacity at gas processing); major operational projects (Sleipner 1996, Snøhvit, Quest, Boundary Dam, Gorgon, Petrobras Santos 10.6 Mt/yr, Northern Lights Phase 1 Aug 2025, Brevik cement 2025, Stratos Texas DAC 2025, Moomba, Net Zero Teesside UK FID 2024, ExxonMobil Baytown PAUSED Nov 2025); economic drivers (post-OBBBA 45Q $85 EOR-storage parity, $180 DAC; UK CCS-CfD; EU Innovation Fund €38B); five substantive critiques (fossil-fuel lifeline, EOR doesn't count, capital displacement, MRV insufficient, cost curve not bending); path forward 2026–2030 (optimistic/central/pessimistic scenarios).
- **Chapter 11: CDR.** Full draft, ~7,000 words. Seven parts: the CDR concept (distinct from CCS); conventional vs. novel CDR; DAC in depth (S-DAC Climeworks Mammoth 36 ktCO₂/yr; L-DAC 1PointFive Stratos 500 ktCO₂/yr; Heirloom mineralization; emerging electrochemical; current costs $400–800 → 2030 target $250–350); BECCS in depth (Stockholm Exergi FID 2024 for 800 ktCO₂/yr; Drax UK pending; biomass sustainability question; IPCC reliance on BECCS); broader removal portfolio (enhanced rock weathering, ocean alkalinity, biochar, biomass burial, mineralization); demand side (Microsoft as 80% of demand; Frontier $1B+ AMC; SBTi V2 mandatory removals; EU CRCF formalized Dec 2024; sovereign Article 6 demand); substantive critiques (moral hazard, energy/land demands, permanence/verification, cost trajectory, geographic concentration); 2026–2050 trajectory (optimistic 5–8 GtCO₂/yr novel CDR vs. central 2–4 vs. pessimistic 0.5–1.5).
- **Chapter 12: Hard-to-Abate Sectors.** Full draft, ~6,600 words. Seven parts: what makes sectors hard-to-abate (process emissions, high-temp heat, long capital stock, trade exposure, limited substitution); steel deep dive (BF-BOF vs. EAF chemistry; H₂-DRI pathway; Stegra 2.5 Mt/yr ramping; 2025–2026 retrenchment with Thyssenkrupp/ArcelorMittal postponements; MOE alternative chemistries); cement deep dive (calcination chemistry; clinker substitution; SCMs; alternative cements; CCS-led approach; Brevik operational 2025); chemicals and refining (green/blue ammonia; methanol; ethylene electrification; bio-based); aviation (SAF pathways; ReFuelEU mandate 2%→6%→70%; UK SAF Mandate; Singapore SAF levy 2027; hydrogen aircraft pre-commercial; cost premiums 200–400%); shipping (IMO 2025 agreement landmark $100–$380/tCO₂ entering force 2027; methanol/ammonia alternatives; wind-assist); policy architecture (green premiums; First Movers Coalition; CBAM as sectoral lever; hub-based deployment); 2026–2030 sectoral outlook.
- **Chapter 7: Compliance Carbon Markets.** Full draft, ~7,300 words. Seven parts covering cap-and-trade fundamentals; the EU ETS in depth (Phases 1-4, ETS2, 2026 reform); the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM, full enforcement Q1 2026 at €75.36/tCO₂e); North American systems (California Cap-and-Invest extended to 2045 in Sept 2025, RGGI, Washington); other major systems (China national ETS, UK ETS with pending EU linkage, Korea, others); the case for and against cap-and-trade; and cap-and-trade vs. carbon taxes.
- **Chapter 8: Voluntary Carbon Markets.** Full draft, ~7,000 words. Seven parts covering VCM fundamentals and how it differs from compliance; the project lifecycle and four integrity concerns (additionality, baseline, permanence, leakage); the major standards (Verra, Gold Standard, ART, ACR, CAR, Isometric, Puro.earth, Plan Vivo); the 2022–2024 crisis (Guardian/Die Zeit/SourceMaterial investigation, cookstove crisis, wildfire reversals); supply-side reform via ICVCM and Core Carbon Principles; demand-side reform via VCMI Claims Code and SBTi V2; current state of prices, demand, and unresolved questions about scale-vs-quality and Article 6.
- **Chapter 5: Emissions Accounting — The Rosetta Stone.** Full draft, ~8,600 words. Seven parts covering Scope 1/2/3 architecture, organizational boundaries, inventories vs. targets vs. claims, financed and facilitated emissions (PCAF), the avoided/reduced/removed distinction, current status of all major standards (GHG Protocol revisions, ISSB IFRS S1/S2 adoption, PCAF 3rd edition Dec 2025, SBTi CNZS V2 second consultation Nov 2025), and a worked guide to reading a corporate climate disclosure.
- **Chapter 6: Carbon Policy History, 1988–2015.** Full draft, ~10,500 words. Seven parts tracing the institutional pathway from climate science (1979 World Climate Conference, IPCC formation 1988, FAR 1990) through UNFCCC (1992), Kyoto (1997, including the Byrd-Hagel Resolution and US withdrawal in 2001), the CDM and EU ETS launch (2005), Copenhagen's collapse (2009), Cancún and Durban rebuilding (2010–2011), to Paris (2015). Explicit treatment of the Annex I / non-Annex I "firewall" and its political dissolution.
- **Chapter 4: Fossil Fuels and the Carbon Stock We're Burning.** Full draft, ~9,500 words. Six parts covering: what coal/oil/gas physically are; the global oil market (OPEC+, IOCs, NOCs, shale); the regional gas markets plus LNG bridge plus methane leakage; the coal market and China-dominance dynamic; the reserves vs. carbon-budget mismatch and stranded-asset thesis; and a critical reading of the IEA's 2025 World Energy Outlook including the CPS reintroduction.
- **Chapter 3: The CO₂ Molecule Value Chain.** Full draft covering the physical infrastructure for moving CO₂ — sources, capture-and-storage vs. merchant-CO₂ value chains, US CO₂-EOR legacy (~5,000 miles of pipeline, ~70 Mt/yr), the 45Q/IRA/OBBBA economic shifts, the Satartia pipeline rupture and PHMSA's January 2025 NPRM, and the 2025–2030 hub landscape (North Sea, US Gulf Coast, Alberta, Midwest ethanol corridor).
- **Chapter 2: Solid Carbon as Commodity.** Full draft covering metallurgical/petroleum coke, carbon black, activated carbon, natural and synthetic graphite, carbon fiber, and the smaller commodity-carbon markets. Includes the China graphite export-licensing story and the IRA/CBAM response context.
